Grid bottlenecks and new rules shape data-centre supply

Slow grid connections are among the bottlenecks facing data-centre growth, alongside tighter equipment supply chains and strained planning and regulatory systems, the IEA says. Connection queues are large and under scrutiny, and authorities from Texas to Lisbon are changing the rules on connections and permits. This briefing compares our three regions, using figures checked against the linked sources.

The big picture

The International Energy Agency says electricity demand from data centres rose 17% in 2025 and is set to double by 2030. It reports that, "constrained by slow grid connections", developers are turning to onsite gas-fired generation, largely in the United States. It also says supply chains for gas turbines, transformers, advanced chips and IT components "have tightened over the past year", and that the pipeline of data-centre projects "is straining planning and regulatory systems" (IEA news release on Key Questions on Energy and AI, 2026).

Connection queues are large. ERCOT, the Texas grid operator, said in June it was tracking more than 438 GW (438,000 MW) of large-load requests, nearly 89% of them from data centres (ERCOT, 18 Jun 2026). In Italy, according to Terna, requests for grid connections for new data centres exceeded 100 GW as at 31 August, almost half in Lombardy (Terna data via Il Sole 24 Ore, 4 Nov 2026). Both are requests for a grid connection, not built capacity or IT load, and their scope differs: ERCOT counts all large loads, Terna only new data centres.

United States

Vacancy is at a record low. In CBRE's eight primary North American markets, it fell to 1.4% in H1 2026, from 1.6% a year earlier. Capacity under construction rose 24.8% to a record 7,481.1 MW, with 80.4% of it already committed, and less than 1,500 MW of future capacity remained available. Average rents for 3 to 10 MW deployments rose 8.3% in the half (CBRE, North America Data Center Trends H1 2026, Aug 2026). In Northern Virginia, vacancy was 0.2%. "Power is the deciding factor in Northern Virginia right now," said Stu Dyer of CBRE (CBRE, 8 Sep 2026).

The strain shows on the grid. PJM's capacity auction for the 2028/2029 delivery year cleared at the price cap of $325/MW-day and fell 6,831 MW short of the reliability requirement. PJM says the auction also saw "the continued trend of the addition of large data center loads to the load forecast that forms the basis of the reliability requirement" (PJM, 14 Jul 2026). On 18 June, FERC gave the six regional grid operators it oversees 60 days to justify or reform their rules for connecting data centres and other large loads, including on co-location and on preventing cost shifting (FERC, 18 Jun 2026).

In Texas, after a directive from Governor Abbott on 3 August calling for "a comprehensive verification and audit", ERCOT paused approvals to energise new data centres and crypto-mining facilities of 75 MW or more (ERCOT, 20 Aug 2026). On 3 September, ERCOT gave provisional classifications for Batch Zero, its one-time transitional study of the reliability impacts of large loads: 204 projects totalling 66.4 GW met the qualifications to be conditionally included as base load and 158 as studied load, while 373 projects totalling 302.2 GW were excluded from the batch, a decision developers may dispute. Final inclusion or exclusion will be based on a verification and audit of the eligibility of the projects provisionally included (ERCOT, Sep 2026). On 5 November, ERCOT said that, although it had not completed its review, common issues were emerging, including site control and the delivery of long-lead equipment before energisation, and that it must report to the PUCT on 10 December (ERCOT, 5 Nov 2026).

Gulf

We found no official Gulf data on vacancy or rents, so these figures describe capacity, electricity use and projects. Saudi Arabia's communications ministry says the Kingdom expanded data-centre capacity to 440 MW in 2025, without saying whether this is IT or facility capacity (MCIT, Annual Report 2025). HUMAIN, a PIF company, and DataVolt plan to develop 100 MW of a 360 MW first phase at Oxagon in NEOM, within a planned 1.5 GW campus, with the first 100 MW expected to be available in 2028 (NEOM, 31 Aug 2026).

In the UAE, Wood Mackenzie estimates that data centres used 3 TWh of electricity in 2025, about 2% of national demand, and expects over 6 TWh by 2030. It says regulatory gaps constrain clean-energy procurement: "UAE regulations do not permit corporate or virtual power purchase agreements" (Wood Mackenzie, 26 Mar 2026).

Southern Europe

In Italy, the industry association IDA puts installed IT capacity at about 760 MW at the end of 2025, of which 460 MW commercial. Its qualified pipeline is about 1,800 MW, with 230 MW under construction (IDA data via Teleborsa, 8 Oct 2026). Requests and IT capacity are different measures. Cushman & Wakefield sets them side by side, "more than 100 GW of grid connection requests, yet only around 460 MW of commercial capacity currently in operation", and says many requests are speculative (Cushman & Wakefield, 9 Nov 2026).

Rules are changing too. Decree-Law 21/2026, converted into Law 49/2026, introduced a single authorisation procedure for data centres and their connection infrastructure (Cushman & Wakefield, 9 Nov 2026; Camera dei deputati). Lombardy's regional law 11/2026, in force since 20 June, treats data centres above 5 MW as productive use and those up to 5 MW as also compatible with tertiary and office uses. It doubles the construction contribution on agricultural land and bans drawing water from aqueducts and drinking or irrigation supplies (Assolombarda). The enterprise ministry, MIMIT, has sent regions, municipalities and IDA a proposal for national guidelines, to be assessed in a conferenza dei servizi (Teleborsa, 27 Nov 2026). Related analysis in Italian: CRE Insider Italia, 28 Nov 2026.

In Spain, three ministries opened an urgent public hearing on 27 August on a draft Royal Decree. It would set requirements for installations above 1 MW, require at least 80% of the electricity consumed to be renewable energy generated in the same hour, and make compliance a condition for grid access and connection rights. The ministries cite Spain's Artificial Intelligence Strategy, which estimates about 2.5 GW of computing power in 2030, needing 3.5 to 4 GW of electricity demand (Government of Spain, 27 Aug 2026). Red Eléctrica reported in February that the transmission grid still had capacity for new demand, of any type, at 25% of its nodes, and that none of the 11.8 GW granted for new demand was yet in service (Red Eléctrica, 20 Feb 2026).

Portugal's Council of Ministers has approved a National Data Center Plan. It makes AICEP the single point of contact for investors, sets maximum licensing deadlines and draws on rules allowing unused grid capacity to be reallocated to high-demand projects (AICEP Portugal Global, 20 Apr 2026).

What it means for real estate

Three points stand out in the sources, without implying any view on prices. First, Cushman & Wakefield says that in Italy "a site with available power, a confirmed operator, and an advanced permitting process is far more valuable than land backed solely by a grid connection request" (Cushman & Wakefield, 9 Nov 2026). Second, a request is not a pipeline, as ERCOT's provisional Batch Zero classification shows; its audit of the projects provisionally included is due to report on 10 December. Third, the rules now reach into zoning and water, as in Lombardy, and Spain's draft decree would add energy sourcing. IDA reports that about 80% of the surface area of the projects it analysed is already urbanised or disused land (IDA data via Teleborsa, 8 Oct 2026). The open questions for any site are whether power is secured, when it will be energised and whether permits meet the new rules.

What to watch

  • ERCOT's report to the PUCT, and whether the energisation pause is lifted.
  • FERC's rulings on the grid operators' responses to its June orders.
  • The final text of Spain's Royal Decree on data centres.
  • The conferenza dei servizi on MIMIT's guidelines, and Terna's next update on connection requests.
