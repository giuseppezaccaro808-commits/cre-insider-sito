Cross-border capital leans south and east as US credit tightens

In late 2026, US commercial property credit is getting tighter. Volumes are strong in Italy, Spain and the Gulf, and foreign buyers account for a large share of them in Italy, Dubai and Abu Dhabi. This first Sourced CRE briefing compares the three markets, using figures checked against their original sources.

The big picture

The three markets are in different phases. In the United States, borrowing costs are high and banks are tightening lending, while delinquency trends are mixed. In Italy and the Gulf, the published data show foreign capital as a large source of liquidity. Our sources do not show money moving out of the US and into these markets, so we draw no causal link. Where demand in 2026 has been strong in Italy and the Gulf, foreign buyers account for a large share of it. In Spain, domestic capital made up close to half of first-half volume.

Across Europe, investment reached €51.5bn in Q3 2026. That was 4% lower than a year earlier, but the year to date is up 8%, the strongest first three quarters since 2022. Activity was split "almost evenly between domestic and cross-border investors" (CBRE, 5 Nov 2026). The ECB says CRE prime yield spreads have "narrowed markedly" and are "close to levels last seen in 2022". It also says transaction activity "remains subdued but stable" (ECB, Financial Stability Review, 18 Nov 2026).

United States

The 10-year Treasury yield was 5.10% on 17 November (Federal Reserve Board via FRED, 18 Nov 2026). The upper bound of the federal funds target range was 4.00% (Federal Reserve Board via FRED, 18 Nov 2026). The Fed puts the average CRE capitalisation rate at 6.79%, using monthly data through August, and says cap rates "have increased sizably from historical lows in 2022" (Federal Reserve Board, Financial Stability Report, Nov 2026).

Credit conditions have changed direction. The Fed's Financial Stability Report says lending standards on new CRE loans "continued to ease in the first two quarters of 2026". In the October survey of bank loan officers, however, domestic banks reported tighter standards and weaker demand across all CRE loan categories in Q3 (Federal Reserve Board, SLOOS, Oct 2026).

  • CMBS delinquency, meaning loan balances 30 or more days overdue, was 4.97% in Q3, up from 4.82% the quarter before. Delinquency on life insurer loans was 1.33%, up from 1.19% (MBA, 27 Oct 2026).
  • KBRA's own measure of CMBS delinquency was 7.7% in October, down 2 basis points on the month. Its office distress rate rose 11 basis points (KBRA, 30 Oct 2026).
  • Delinquency on bank CRE loans was 1.53% in Q2 2026, down from 1.56% in Q1 (Federal Reserve Board via FRED, 25 Aug 2026).
  • National office vacancy was 18.2% in Q3, down 10 basis points (CBRE, 23 Oct 2026).

The Fed also warns that "a substantial volume" of non-agency CMBS debt "is set to mature in late 2026". It adds that lenders' willingness to extend or restructure loans "has helped mitigate this risk" (Federal Reserve Board, Financial Stability Report, Nov 2026).

Gulf

Dubai's transactions reached AED252bn in Q1 2026, up 31% year on year. Foreign investment was AED148.35bn, up 26%, and 29,312 investors entered the market for the first time (Dubai Land Department, 9 Apr 2026). These follow a 2025 total of AED917bn, up 20% (Government of Dubai Media Office, 12 Jan 2026). In H1 2026, 104 projects were completed, up 38.7%, adding 24,537 units (Dubai Land Department, 7 Sep 2026). Real estate activities made up 11.2% of Dubai's GDP in Q1 (Government of Dubai Media Office, 8 Jul 2026).

In Abu Dhabi, transactions in the first nine months reached AED171.26bn, up 76.1%. Foreign investment in investment zones was AED86.11bn, up 131% (Abu Dhabi Media Office / ADREC, 7 Nov 2026).

Two limits apply. None of the official sources we checked splits out offices or commercial property. We also found no official Dubai total for any period after Q1 2026. Totals for later periods are circulating in the press, but we could not trace them to an official source and have left them out.

Southern Europe

Italy recorded €2.8bn of investment in Q3, up 14%, and €10.6bn for the year to date, up 36%. Foreign capital accounted for 72% of Q3 volume and 81% of hospitality (Cushman & Wakefield, MarketBeat Italy Investment Q3 2026). Dils puts Q3 at about €3.1bn and growth in the first nine months at 30%. It also reports that US institutional capital has returned (Dils, 8 Oct 2026). Italian non-residential prices were "sostanzialmente stabili" (broadly stable) in Q2, and the number of sales rose 4.2% (Banca d'Italia, Rapporto sulla stabilità finanziaria 2/2026, Nov 2026).

Dils says Italy and Spain each recorded their "highest-ever performance" for the first nine months (Dils, 2 Nov 2026). The latest Spanish figures we have checked at source cover H1 2026: investment above €12bn, up 59%, with domestic capital "close to 50%" (CBRE, 29 Jul 2026). Portugal took €622m in Q3, down 6%, and €2.0bn over nine months, up 6% (CBRE, 28 Oct 2026).

What to watch

  • The FDIC Quarterly Banking Profile for Q3 and the Q3 figure for bank CRE loan delinquency.
  • The late-2026 CMBS maturities, and whether lenders keep extending loans now that the October survey shows tighter standards.
  • Primary-source publication of CBRE's Q3 figures for Spain, which we could not open before going to press.
  • Official releases from Dubai Land Department for 2026 beyond Q1, and any official split of commercial or office deals.
  • How the narrower prime yield spreads the ECB describes affect European deal volumes.
